Does a network output torques?NoIt outputs one number per joint, turned into a target angle.
Does it output trajectories?NoA fresh target every 20 ms. The network's memory (a GRU) carries the motion from cycle to cycle.
Does it talk to the PD controller?YesEach message sets, for all 23 joints, the target angle, target speed 0, kp, kd and extra torque 0. The server's PD turns that into torque.
Who sets the gains?We doMovement and kick: kp 25 N·m/rad, kd 0.6 on every joint, as in training. Get-up: kp = the joint's torque cap per radian, for full strength.

Training loop

1 · Simulate8,192 T1 robots at once on one GPU with the server's robot model, contacts and one-cycle action delay
→
2 · UpdatePPO: 8,192 × 128 steps = 1,048,576 steps per iteration, 10 epochs
→
3 · Curriculumpenalties, shoves and randomisation phase in as the robots succeed
→
4 · League testsexport to C++, fixed test batteries in the real server; only a pass replaces the final

The three skills together

The player runs one network at a time and switches on its own: get-up whenever the robot is down, movement to reach the ball, kick once it is lined up behind the ball. Times are from the robot's arrival on the field.

The orchestrator: who does what, every 20 ms

rules layer: no trained parametersRL tactics layer: 80,131 parametersruns on every robot, no central computer

The orchestrator never touches a joint. It chooses which skill network runs and gives the movement network its 3-number command (forward, sideways, turn rate). The skill network makes the joint targets; the server's PD makes the torques.

Each cycle, on each robot

Readserver message: joints, gyro, exact torso pose, camera (every 2nd cycle), team talk, game state
Ballown camera sighting, else the freshest teammate report under 1 s old; velocity from 0.4 s of sightings; where it will stop (decelerates 2.3 m/s²)
Fallen?torso under 0.30 m or tilted over 45° → get-up network until standing is held 0.5 s; fresh retry after 10 s
Rolefirst match in this order: WAIT (before kick-off, after a goal) → KICK-OFF spots → AWAY at their restarts → STRIKER (lowest cost) → KEEPER (#1) → SUPPORT
Commanda target point → goto law → movement network; or the kick network when the striker is lined up
Talkon its own cycle of 7: a 10-byte message to the team

Who plays the ball: cost auction

cost = d / 3 m/s + 0.5 · |heading error| + 1.5 (if on the far side of the ball) + 3 (if fallen)

d = distance to the kick spot, 0.8 m behind the ball on the aim line. Lowest cost plays; the current striker gets −0.7 s so it does not flip; ties go to the lower number. Keeper only inside its own penalty area. A robot that took a restart sits out 8 s (no double touch).

When the striker kicks

ConditionValue

Aim: the goal centre, or 1 m to the side away from a keeper standing in the goal mouth. Kick-offs and indirect free kicks: the deepest direction (5° scan) whose 30 m roll stays in the field, because a direct goal from them does not count.

Goto law: the command for the movement network

v = min(8, √(2 · 3 · (d − 0.125))) m/s
v ≤ 1 m/s while the target is more than 30° off
turn rate = clamp(3 · bearing, ±2 rad/s)
within 1 m: sideways steps, no turning

Brakes so it stops on the point. Arrived within 0.3 m; moves again only past 0.6 m. Support robots re-aim only when their spot moves over 0.8 m, and face the ball.

Team talk: 10 bytes

tag 5b · #3b
own x, y · 12 bits each
ball x, y · 12 bits each
ball age
cost ×10
fallen, striker, check 6b

Each robot speaks on cycle number mod 7, so one teammate per cycle. The check bits are salted with a hash of the team name, so a copy of our player on the other side cannot pass as a teammate.

Highlight reels: every goal of every match

Each reel runs the whole match: open play fast-forwarded 8 times (marked x8), then real speed from 8 s before each goal to 3 s after it. Sides swap at half time, and the server colours the left team blue and the right team red, so our colour changes with the half.

The catch

  • FC Portugal and magmaOffenburg are their Portuguese Open 2026 binaries (archive.robocup.info), run on our machine. They are not necessarily the teams they will bring to RoboCup 2026.
  • FC Portugal's binary lost two of its seven players in each half (its own players exited mid-match), so 9-0 and 10-0 were against five to seven of their robots. magmaOffenburg kept all seven: 11-0 is the cleanest result.
  • All matches but the Apollo one ran in the server's synchronous mode, where it waits for every player each step. The Apollo match ran in real time, as in competition.
  • One match per opponent so far. Our robots still fall 32 to 48 times a match, mostly on contact and after the referee teleports a robot that stood too near the ball at the other side's restart.
  • The learned layer's gain in the simulator has not yet shown up in the league server.
